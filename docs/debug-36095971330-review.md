## Ladder coverage and fits

The six ratios are 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1, 1.5 and 2. Each displayed option
order is monotone over this range: **zero observed keep-to-give decreases**
as Sam's payoff increases. Monotonicity within an order does not imply
agreement between orders or an identified common switch point.

Pooling the two option orders, while keeping numerical evidence orders
separate, gives thirteen ladders: **nine left-censored, four with tied best
fits, and zero uniquely identified interior estimates**.

Bounds here are threshold-fit outputs, not confidence intervals. Tied ranges
span equally good candidate gaps; none is a precise WTR estimate. The
scorer permits an unbounded lower endpoint and does not silently replace
censoring with zero. The full rung-by-rung table remains in the original
[report](../results/debug/36095971330/responses.md).

The primary scorer additionally pools numerical evidence orders. Its nine
main cells then contain **six left-censored fits and three tied fits**, still
with zero interior point estimates. In particular, pooling LOW/T2 yields a
left-censored fit with two violations, concealing the broader ambiguity of
the swapped-history ladder unless the separate-order table is also read.

On numerical-history items, Sam keeps in **94/96 responses**. Both LOW-minus-HIGH
comparisons are undetermined because their fitted intervals overlap. This
is neither evidence of equal latent valuations nor evidence of the intended
LOW-below-HIGH effect. Under a deterministic constant-weight model, the
histories constrain upper bounds; they do not supply known social truth
keys or require the posterior weight to equal the most diagnostic ratio.

## Attribution, ability and the conditional-question problem

The existing scorer finds both intended attribution contrasts in the one
boxes scenario:

- Unable minus unwilling valuation is positive by fitted bounds: unable's
  lower bound is 1, while unwilling's upper bound is 0.1. The unable fit
  still has tied gaps and two violations; there is no point difference.
- Same-task ability is higher for unwilling (yes/yes) than unable (no/no),
  a difference of 1 in the two-response proportions.

These are exploratory patterns in a single scenario, not confirmation of
a general dissociation, validity of a scalar WTR measure, or access to a
shared internal valuation variable.

The willingness readout raises an additional construct problem. In both
unable/same-task prompts, the future question explicitly states:
“Sam is free that day and would be able to do it.” Both responses predict
declining and explain this using an unchanged bad shoulder. That explanation
does not respect the supplied future-ability condition. The text combines
an earlier physical limitation with a later capability stipulation, so a
clearer conditional is worth testing. This is an explanation/prompt-interpretation
finding; it does not assign a new truth key to whether Sam would agree.

In the unwilling/different-task ability mismatch, the question stipulates
a real effort with enough time. The no response instead cites declining
or failing to follow through; the reversed-order yes response acknowledges
the stipulated effort. This suggests the intended separation of motivation
from ability is not reliably maintained in these outputs. No answer is
rescored from its explanation.

## Decision and bounded next step

**Keep the 888-item pilot unrun, unfrozen and unregistered. End identical
calibration repetitions and do not repeat the same 220-item batch as the
default next action.** Collection now works across all probe families, but
more requests on the unchanged design would not by themselves resolve the
coverage and interpretation problems.

The next useful work is to specify one bounded measurement diagnostic
before further calls, using already exposed debug cases:

1. Test coverage below 0.1 and above 2 while retaining selected original
   rungs, both displayed orders, and the affected numerical history orders.
   Keep original items as anchors. A wider ladder can locate a switch or
   show continued censoring; it cannot guarantee a common switch across
   orders. Do not densify only until a preferred social ordering appears.
2. Make the conditional ability/effort stipulations explicit in matched
   versions of the affected binary probes, retaining the original wording
   as a comparison. Specify what counts as an explanation contradicting
   the condition independently of the final social judgment.
3. Keep the same model and explanation/A-B protocol, separate known-answer
   controls, and a fixed item list and budget. Report persistent censoring,
   order disagreement and absent theoretical effects as possible outcomes.

This is a design recommendation, **not a configured or dispatched next
workflow**. Before implementation, resolve the numerical representation:
the current fixed amount of 10 supports integer payoffs only in increments
of 0.1. Smaller ratios would require fractional points or a larger fixed
amount. Either changes the representation or stakes and needs explicit
matched anchors; simply rounding smaller rungs would create zero or
duplicate payoffs. Avoid conflating a stakes change with improved resolution.
Set the request count and stopping rule before seeing those new responses.

Pilot readiness should depend on whether the measure supports its intended
comparisons with interpretable bounds and acceptable robustness, not on
obtaining the hypothesized LOW/HIGH or unable/unwilling ordering. A stable
null or contrary pattern can be a legitimate result. If the bounded
diagnostic still cannot distinguish these possibilities, narrow the study
to documented response robustness rather than presenting an unvalidated
latent valuation estimate.

## Implications for the article

This run supports a concrete methods finding: perfect performance on these
known-answer controls and consistent answers on the small calibration
subset did not guarantee robustness or informative valuation estimates
on the larger debug battery. The broader audit also caught a conditional
interpretation issue that final-answer parsing could not detect.
That is useful research evidence when reported with all runs and limitations.
It does not yet justify claiming that WTR-Bench measures a general social
valuation mechanism, that the model lacks one, or that the planned pilot
has been completed.
