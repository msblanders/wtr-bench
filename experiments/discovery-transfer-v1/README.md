# Discovery and independent-game transfer, v1

Prospective pilot protocol. This study extends `cue-transfer-v1`; it does not change that study or reuse its trained weights. Freeze this file, all executable sources, dependencies, seed plan, and the external-source checksums before selecting any study model. Preserve every completed replicate, including failures and unfavorable results. No API calls.

## Questions and decision rule

Can population selection discover useful within-lifetime updating without supplied willingness/ability routes? Does the resulting inherited procedure still use experience successfully in games generated by an independent implementation?

Three **joint primary criteria**, all evaluated at the final development-selected checkpoint and averaged equally over histories of 4, 12, and 24 episodes:

1. In the held-out `shifted` synthetic suite, the evolved model predicts observed future outcomes better with memory updating than with its state held at zero.
2. In that same suite, evolution beats independent random search with the same candidate-fitness budget and same architecture.
3. In `external_games`, the frozen evolved model predicts observed future choices better with updating than with its state held at zero.

All three paired run-level bootstrap 95% intervals must have strictly positive lower bounds before reporting joint pilot success. The contrast is comparator loss minus evolved intact loss. These are a conjunction, not three opportunities to declare a win. Intervals are descriptive uncertainty from eight independent selection/training runs, not a population-wide discovery guarantee. No result-dependent stopping or retry. If selection fails while matched gradient training succeeds, the bounded search was inadequate; this does not refute the existence or evolvability of the procedure under other budgets. If both fail, representation, objective, exposure and optimization remain possible explanations. If the external criterion fails, do not claim transfer beyond our simulator.

The shared dense learner and the exact-architecture gradient control are important comparative benchmarks, not required losers. Evolution need not beat gradient optimization to establish that selection can discover a learning procedure. If a dense shared learner succeeds, functional differentiation remains possible inside shared weights. No separate-module requirement is imposed.

## Development environments and observable interface

Each lifetime concerns one new target. Persistent state resets to zero at its start. A target has an independent social weight rho and execution probability a, both Uniform(0,1). There are three public context symbols; histories occur in two, with the third held out. All three symbols and all query contexts occur across training lifetimes. Training queries draw a context uniformly; evaluation queries use the lifetime's held-out context. Context identity is not an environment-family label.

A voluntary episode offers two actions: do nothing `(0,0)` or act with own payoff `-cost` and recipient payoff `benefit`. Choice probabilities are softmax of `own + rho * other`, with inverse temperature 1. Assigned episodes force the nonzero action. Only an attempted action can produce an execution outcome, Bernoulli(a); 20% of attempted outcomes are unobserved. An omitted outcome is explicitly marked as unobserved, not presented as a failure. The inherited model sees instruction, actual action/payoffs, outcomes and their availability; it never sees rho, a or the habitat identity.

Three development habitats occur equally often, without labels:

| Habitat | Cost range | Benefit range | Assignment probabilities in contexts 0,1,2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Uniform(.2,1.2) | Uniform(2,4) | .15, .85, .5 |
| 1 | Uniform(.5,2) | Uniform(.5,3) | .5, .5, .5 |
| 2 | Uniform(0,.8) | Uniform(1,2) | .85, .15, .5 |

Training histories are padded to 24 and have lengths sampled equally from 0,4,12,24. A training example has two fresh **observed** targets: a future voluntary choice from a new menu, and a future success/failure given a guaranteed attempt. Future outcomes are conditionally independent draws, never copied from the history. Payoffs here are cues for predicting another actor's choices; the learner itself does not earn or maximize game payoffs.

All learners receive the same 15 event features: voluntary instruction; action differs from do-nothing; observed success; chosen own/other payoff; menu mean own/other payoff; menu standard deviation of own/other payoff; covariance; log action count; three context indicators; execution-observed flag. The seven payoff summaries are scaled and transformed with asinh. Query inputs are the context, each available option's scaled/asinh payoffs, and a public choice/execution-query indicator. Option scores share a readout and are normalized over the actual menu; the execution score uses a sigmoid. There is no fixed number of options, option truncation, inferred trait label, evidence-eligibility mask or named memory bank.

This is **not** learning from raw language or discovering a sensory vocabulary: the fixed feature summaries and query meanings are supplied. Menu summaries preserve binary menus but may lose information about a larger menu. Every learned comparator uses those same summaries, so the larger learner does not secretly receive more observation information. The supplied statistical reference receives the full menus and knows a generative form; it is explicitly privileged, not a matched-information architecture comparison.

## Inherited procedures and selection

The small architecture has four initially unnamed scalar states. At each event, the concatenation of all event features, all four prior states and a bias goes through two freely selected affine maps. One produces sigmoid gates, the other tanh proposals. Every coordinate updates as `h + gate * (proposal - h)`. All four coordinates can depend on every cue and on each other. A shared eight-unit tanh readout combines the state with each query; a single linear score feeds either choice softmax or execution sigmoid. The gate/proposal maps and the entire readout are inherited together: **257 selected parameters**, four state values. None has an assigned psychological meaning.

The initial search distribution has mean zero except a domain-independent gate bias of -2, and standard deviation .5. This generic retention bias is also used for matched gradient initialization; it is not an eligibility rule. The evolutionary procedure uses 512 generations, 32 candidates per generation, top eight ranked candidates, and normalized weights proportional to `log(8.5)-log(rank)`. Candidate zero is the current mean. The mean moves .3 toward the weighted elite mean. Standard deviations move .15 toward the weighted elite deviation around the previous mean, clamped to [.05,2]. Thus this is rank-weighted Gaussian population search, not a tournament of prewritten theory rules. No correct solution is inserted. There is no within-lifetime gradient update for this learner; only the inherited recurrent computation updates its state.

Each generation draws 64 new lifetimes, shared by all candidates and all methods. Fitness is the equally weighted average of choice and execution Brier scores against the two observed future outcomes. Categorical choice Brier is `0.5 * sum((p-onehot(y))^2)`, which agrees with binary Brier for two actions. Fitness never uses latent traits, Bayesian posterior targets, cue-rule resemblance, external-game data or diagnostic scores. The genotype budget is 16,384 candidate evaluations, or 1,048,576 candidate-lifetime evaluations, per search method and replicate. There are 32,768 distinct training lifetimes per method.

Comparison methods:

| Method | Procedure | Parameters | Persistent states |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| evolved | Population search over the generic update and readout | 257 | 4 |
| random_search | Independent draws from the fixed initial distribution, same candidate budget | 257 | 4 |
| matched_gradient | Exactly the small architecture, Adam learns all inherited weights | 257 | 4 |
| dense_gradient | GRU over the same event features, shared 16-unit query readout | 1969 | 16 |

At every generation the evolved distribution mean is evaluated on a fixed 256-lifetime development set. For random search, the candidate with the best current training-batch fitness receives that development evaluation. Each keeps its best development candidate so far. This specifies the random-search algorithm fully; it is not exhaustive development scoring of every random candidate. Both begin with the same independently drawn initial candidate. Development data use the same habitat mixture as training and are distinct from training and all final evaluation seeds.

Gradient methods use 512 Adam updates, learning rate .003, gradient norm cap 1, the same sequence of training batches, the same fixed development data, and best-development selection after each update. The small gradient learner has exactly the shared initial candidate's parameters. Dense initialization uses its standard PyTorch initialization under the same replicate seed. No weight decay, early stopping, tuning sweep or extra exposure. The checkpoints are 0,128,512; each represents the best development model observed by that point. Full final external evaluation is produced only after all four methods finish training. Within a replicate the search runs jointly first; gradient order alternates across replicates. CPU one-thread deterministic PyTorch, pinned dependencies.

Exposure is matched, **optimization work is not**: population search scores 32 candidates per generation; gradient training performs a backward pass; the dense model has more parameters and memory. Report wall time and parameter/state/MAC counts; do not claim equal-FLOP superiority or a data-to-criterion speed ratio from this pilot. Candidate validation reuse may overfit the development set; final test worlds are independent and never select a checkpoint.

## Held-out environments

512 fresh targets per suite, per history length (4,12,24,48), per replicate. Each length has independently generated lifetimes; all methods see identical data for that cell. The 48-episode results test longer lifetimes and are secondary. No target-specific memory persists between lifetimes. The inherited weights remain frozen during every evaluation, while within-lifetime state updating is allowed.

| Suite | Change relative to development | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| canonical | Fresh targets and draws, same habitat mixture | Within-distribution check |
| shifted | Costs Uniform(1,3), benefits Uniform(1,5), assignment .05 in habitat 0 and .95 otherwise | Primary synthetic generalization |
| local_preferences | Independent rho in each context | Boundary: pooling preferences into an unseen context is wrong |
| correlated_traits | rho equals a | Boundary: execution now indirectly informs preference |
| preference_reversal | rho becomes 1-rho halfway through each test history | Boundary: old evidence becomes stale |
| external_games | Pinned upstream Game Generator and built-in actors | Primary external implementation transfer, choice only |

Boundary suites deliberately violate development assumptions. They characterize limits; a loss there is not silently dropped. A rule that ignores assigned execution for preference can be sensible in development and inappropriate when traits correlate. This pilot does not select in those new ecologies, so it cannot establish that selection differentiates the rules appropriately across ecologies; that requires a later train-ecology comparison.

### Independent implementation

Use [maxkw/evolution](https://github.com/maxkw/evolution), the code associated with *Evolving general cooperation with a Bayesian theory of mind* (Kleiman-Weiner et al., PNAS 2025; [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2400993122)). Pin commit `8d7f9946b3cc4ff1d7fae7a1a7a149499c35dede`. Download unchanged `games.py`, `agents.py`, `utils.py`, verify their SHA-256 values recorded in `external.py`, and retain them in the frozen run artifact. This repository stores our adapter and provenance rather than republishing upstream source.

The adapter executes the original AST definitions of `Playable`, `Decision`, `engine_gen`, `softmax`, `add_tremble`, `AgentType`, `Agent`, `SelfishAgent` and `AltruisticAgent`. It does not rewrite those definitions; it avoids importing unrelated plotting/evolution orchestration. Legacy RNG state is seeded and restored per menu. Pin NumPy 1.26.4 for compatibility with upstream `np.NaN`/`np.Inf`.

Generate menus by calling upstream `engine_gen(nactions=3,max_players=2,benefit=5,cost=1,tremble=.02)`. This samples one plus a Poisson number of nonzero options, Poisson costs, and exponential benefit increments. Keep every generated option, including zero-cost actions. Two players retain the existing target/recipient interface. Draw targets equally in expectation from the upstream selfish and altruistic agent classes, inverse temperature 1. Obtain their exact upstream action-likelihood vectors including .02 tremble and sample actions from those probabilities. Their population is bimodal rather than the training continuum. All history choices are voluntary. Execution outcomes are absent and marked unobserved; the execution query is not scored externally. Context labels are harmless adapter labels with no effect on upstream behavior.

No external menu or outcome enters training, development checkpoint selection, architecture selection after the run, or stopping. We inspect the external specification to design the interface, so this is not a benchmark hidden from the investigators. It is an independently authored game generator and actor implementation held out from optimization. It is **not** the full Bayesian Reciprocator, strategic interaction with an adaptive opponent, empirical human evidence, or transfer to many knowledge domains. Do not claim those results. The social utility family still overlaps with training even though menu generation, actor mixture, option count and tremble differ.

## Reference and organization diagnostics

A supplied functional Bayesian reference uses a uniform 81-point quadrature over rho, updates only from voluntary choices, and uses Beta(1,1) counts of observed executions for a. It assumes shared, independent stationary traits, inverse temperature 1 and no tremble in every suite. It is a reference inductive bias, not an unrestricted Bayes ceiling and not privileged knowledge of the held-out suite. Report its observed-outcome scores alongside models. Exact latent choice/execution probabilities are used only for secondary evaluation errors, never training or model selection.

After final selection, append five evidence variants to 256 independent eight-episode histories and query a standard `(-1,3)` offer in the held-out context. Compare voluntary failure versus refusal, voluntary success versus failure, assigned success versus failure, assigned failure versus unchanged, and refusal versus unchanged. Report choice/execution prediction shifts, their difference from the supplied reference, informative-direction accuracy and absolute null-channel shifts. These are behavioral diagnostics, not evidence that one recovered state equals an IRV. Report null shifts honestly; selection has no hard-coded guarantee of them.

For the final evolved model also clamp each of its four state coordinates to zero after every update, one at a time, on canonical and external 24-episode data. Report both outcome-channel losses when defined. These interventions test coordinate dependence in this implementation; they can be off-manifold and do not identify a unique abstract variable, minimal rank, or physical module. Bank labels are arbitrary. The primary all-state-zero comparator tests whether useful information is carried by learned state updates; it does not by itself establish theory-correct causal organization.

## Analysis, seeds and preservation

Eight independent runs are the replication unit. Pair models within a run; average equally across primary history lengths before comparing. Bootstrap whole paired runs with 10,000 resamples and seed 128000000. Never treat targets, generations, menu options or repeated checkpoints as independent evolutionary replications. Report every run's contrast and all suite means. All secondary comparisons are exploratory and are not additional success gates. Describe functional distinctions separately from the three primary performance criteria.

For replicate r: initialization 121000000+r; training 122000000+r; development 123000000+r; evolution 124000000+r; random search 125000000+r; evaluation 126000000+1000*r+100*suite_index+history_length; diagnostics 127000000+r. Development integrity tests use 131000000-series seeds, never these study streams. Training-input and target hashes must match across all four methods. Preserve frozen sources, upstream checksums, initial/final checkpoint weights, search distributions, development logs, data hashes, every replicate's metrics and aggregate analysis. A single guarded Actions launch performs the study; rerunning the same attempt is rejected. Partial artifacts are retained on failure. Infrastructure repair requires a documented new version/launch, not outcome-based rerunning.

This is a first direct test of outcome-driven selection and external-game transfer. It leaves the larger question—selection over a portfolio of learning procedures for several qualitatively different recurrent problems—open. A successful run licenses the narrower result; it does not establish general AI learning theory, literal welfare-tradeoff representation recovery, or human-like developmental origins.
